# Cricket wicket tracker

Personal project: a phone-camera web app for casual/club cricket that decides whether a white ball would have hit the stumps (LBW-style "hitting / missing / umpire's call"), calls wides, and shows ball speed. It also has a separate Scorer mode (tab) for scoring a match by hand.
First trials planned at PA nets, then a pitch to Jersey Cricket.

## How it's built
- Everything lives in a single `index.html` (HTML + CSS + JS inline). No build step, no framework.
- Hosted on Netlify (project "crickettracker", https://cricketracker.netlify.app), auto-deployed from this repo's main branch. Publish directory is the repo root.
- Camera needs https (Netlify provides it). It does not work inside Claude artifact previews.
- Live camera only, by design: there is no video-file option (removed at the user's request). For testing, use Chrome's fake camera with a y4m file.

## Look and layout
- Dark, app-style design (tokens in `:root`: surfaces, lime accent `--accent`, verdict colours). Mobile-first; two columns from 900 px. Sticky app bar with Tracker / Scorer tabs.
- The camera picture (`#cam` > `#stage`) carries glass overlay controls: live fps chip (top left), decision toast (under it), full screen + lock (top right), zoom bar (bottom right), RH / LH batter switch (bottom left). Setup is a sheet (`#setupPanel`) inside `#cam`, so it also works in full screen (bottom sheet in portrait, side panel in landscape phones). While setting up, the toast, RH/LH switch and hints are hidden (and the zoom bar outside full screen).
- Full screen (maximise/minimise): `setFull()` toggles `.cam.full` and uses the Fullscreen API where available (Android, desktop, Mac); on iPhone it is a fixed full-window view. Lock: `setLocked()` ignores touches on the picture, greys zoom/full screen, disables setup buttons, and tries `screen.orientation.lock`. Unlock by holding the lock button 0.7 s.

## How it works
- Camera setup: phone on a stand at the **bowler's end**, behind the bowler's stumps where the umpire stands, looking down the pitch at the batter's stumps. The user enters how far behind the bowler's stumps the phone is (default 2 m); far stumps = 20.12 m + that.
- Setup (redesigned after the user found box-drawing and tapping lines clunky on a phone):
  - One "Set up wicket" button (opens by itself the first time the camera starts). A wicket frame appears at the picture centre, sized for the known distance at the default lens (and camera zoom); the picture magnifies (≤3×) around it.
  - Big controls: a D-pad (1 processing px per tap; hold to repeat, speeding up to 2× and 4×) and − / + (2% size steps). Or drag anywhere on the picture (the frame follows the finger 1:1 on screen, so the finger never covers the stumps) and pinch to resize. Tabs: Wicket / Wides / Batter / Done.
  - Each tab draws real-world guides in perspective (`groundProjector()`, from the wicket's implied camera height and sideways offset): Wicket shows "71.1 cm" / "22.9 cm" dimension lines; Wides shows the batter's-end markings (bowling and popping creases, return creases at 1.32 m, wide guidelines at 0.89 m, 3.05 m pitch edges) with "1.22 m", "0.89 m", "3.05 m pitch" and OFF / LEG arrows; Batter shows a batter outline in the crease on the leg side, for the chosen hand.
  - Wides (`widesNow(hand)`), at the batter's popping crease: off side = the white wide guideline, 0.89 m from the middle stump (placed automatically, since it can't be tapped from 22 m); leg side = automatic, a ball passing more than `LEG_X` 0.2 m outside the middle stump on the leg side is "Wide (leg side)". From the bowler's end a right-hander's off side is picture-right. `S.hand` ('R'/'L') is set by the RH/LH switch or the Batter tab; each delivery stores the hand it was bowled to (`del.hand`) and `judge(pts, hand)` uses it. `wideAdj {dx, g}`: sideways nudge of both lines and the off-side distance scale. Switches: Off-side wides (`S.widesOn`), Leg-side wides (`S.legWides`).
  - Outside setup, touching the picture can't move anything (pinch zooms, drag pans when zoomed). Balls bowled during setup are ignored. Labels keep a fixed on-screen size at any zoom.
  - Optional extras in Detection tuning: Learn ball colour (tap a still ball) and Detection zone.
  - Zoom: + / − buttons (top right of the picture) and pinch. Uses the camera's own `zoom` constraint where available (Chrome on Android), rescaling the wicket (and the wide shift) about the centre, then retakes the drift reference after 700 ms. Elsewhere (e.g. iPhone) it magnifies the picture only. The camera zoom is saved (`S.camZoom`) and restored on start.
- Focal length comes from the drawn wicket and the known distance (`focalProc = stumpsDist × wicket px height / 0.711`), so any camera zoom works. Size limits (minA/maxA) are set from this geometry when the wicket is drawn.
- Ball detection runs on frames downscaled to 960 px on the long side (camera requested at 1920×1080). A candidate is a pixel that is both white (bright, low colour spread) and moving. Each candidate is then grown over the white mask to measure the full ball. If that growth fails (too big or a bad shape, e.g. the ball touching the white crease line), the ball is measured from its moving pixels alone, if that blob is compact.
- Shake tolerance: each frame the global camera shift (±8 px) is estimated against the previous frame (coarse search every 2 px on a 16 px grid, then ±1 px refine on an 8 px grid), and a white pixel only counts as moving if it differs from all 3×3 neighbours of the shifted previous frame. Frames where over 40% of the picture changes are skipped.
- Drift following: a running average of the grey frames (`avg`, factor 0.2) is kept; when the wicket is drawn it becomes the reference. The reference is taken 1.5 s after the camera starts (and 0.7 s after a zoom change). Every 10 frames, except while the user is selecting/adjusting, the running average is matched against it (±24 px, brightness-change tolerant); when the median of the last 3 checks is ≥3 px from the current offset, `off` moves the wicket and wide markers. Averaging matters: single-frame checks with ±2 px shake gave false re-alignments. They are stored in reference coordinates and saved re-based (`wkNow()`/`widesNow()`).
- Frame timing uses the camera's `captureTime` from requestVideoFrameCallback when available. A screen Wake Lock is held while the camera runs, and a camera track that ends shows a restart message.
- Per-frame cost is about 13–17 ms at 960×540 in headless Chrome (the moving-pixel list means candidate search only visits moving white pixels). `window.__wkt().perf` has the timing breakdown.
- Tracking: nearest-neighbour matching with constant-velocity prediction and a size-ratio check; a candidate at a similar speed in any direction is also accepted so tracks survive the bounce (`tr.spd` keeps the speed separately, because averaging velocity vectors across the bounce makes the ball look slow).
- Only deliveries count: the ball must shrink by ≥15% along the track (travelling away towards the far wicket), a wicket must be drawn, and the projected path must head towards the stumps (±8 wicket widths) or, with wide markers, within half a marker-gap outside them. Everything else is silently ignored. The live pink trail is only drawn for tracks that look like a delivery.
- Judging: depth Z(t) is a straight line in time fitted to per-frame size depth (focal × 0.072 / diameter), weighted by diameter⁴ so close sightings dominate (speed must be 5–60 m/s). The bounce is the lowest image point; the post-bounce points (or pre-bounce if too few) are back-projected and fitted with X linear and Y linear + gravity. Solve for Z = far stumps: test against the wicket rectangle with the ball radius as the umpire's-call margin. Wides: same path at the batter's popping crease (1.22 m in front of the far stumps) compared with the markers.
- Speed (`speedOf`): the same size-based depth fit, but only on the points before the bounce (the ball slows on pitching), so it is close to speed out of the hand. No speed is shown if there are fewer than 3 sightings before the bounce or the ball was first seen beyond 60% of the way down the pitch (a ball picked up late gave wrong speeds). Shown on the board, in the pop-up and in the log, in mph by default (tap to switch to km/h, saved as `S.unit`). Valid range 5–60 m/s.
- Settings, wicket, wide-line adjustment and zone are saved in localStorage key `wkt-v3` (v2 was the old camera-behind-the-batter's-stumps layout).
## Scorer mode
- A second tab ("Scorer"), separate from and not connected to the tracker; meant to be used on a different phone. Its own script block at the end of `index.html`.
- New match: team batting first, team bowling first, overs per innings, players per side. Then opening batters + bowler for each innings, names suggested from earlier entries.
- Pad: 0–6 runs, Wicket (how out; run out asks who and runs completed; caught takes an optional fielder; asks the new batter unless it's the last wicket), Wide / No ball / Bye / Leg bye toggles that apply to the next run tap, Undo, Swap strike, Change bowler.
- Rules applied: odd runs swap ends; end of over swaps ends and asks for the next bowler (warns if the same bowler); wides and no-balls add 1 and don't count as balls; no-ball runs off the bat go to the batter; byes/leg byes aren't charged to the bowler; maidens; innings ends at overs, all out (players − 1) or target passed; result "won by N runs / wickets" or tie.
- Event-sourced: a match is `{teams, overs, players, events[]}`; the scoreboard is rebuilt by `replay()`, so Undo pops the last event. Stored in localStorage `wkt-score-v1` (`cur` + up to 30 `past` matches). Scorecard with fall of wickets and economy; Share uses the share sheet or copies text. `window.__score()` for tests. Selected tab is remembered in `wkt-tab`.

## Testing hooks
- `window.__wkt()` exposes deliveries, tracks, overlay positions, selection, view and zoom for automated tests. Multi-touch is tested with CDP `Input.dispatchTouchEvent`; camera zoom by stubbing `MediaStreamTrack` getCapabilities/getSettings/applyConstraints in an init script.

## Status
- Tested only on synthetic camera feeds (headless Chrome with a fake y4m camera, scene rendered from the bowler's end at 960×540 "2× zoom", gravity, bounce, ±2 px shake, textured ground): speeds 20.8 / 30.8 / 36.7 m/s vs 20 / 30 / 36 true (about 1–2 mph high), impact within a few cm sideways and ~7 cm in height; correct hitting/missing/wide, including leg-side wides and swapped sides for left-handers; balls thrown back or rolling across are ignored. Not yet tested on real footage.
- Expected real-world issues: the ball is only a few pixels across near the far wicket (use 2× zoom); white pads, shoes and clothing (bowler's run-up, batter) as false positives; dusk or floodlight lighting; processing speed on older phones (about 30 fps in headless Chrome after the speed-ups). Below ~25 fps the few frames after the bounce can be missed and the impact height falls back to the pre-bounce path (less reliable).

## Working conventions
- Keep it a single self-contained file unless there's a strong reason to split it.
- Test changes before pushing. A fake-camera test (Playwright + `--use-file-for-fake-video-capture`) worked well; generate y4m clips of the scene with a small Node script.
- Commit with clear messages and push to main. Netlify deploys automatically.
